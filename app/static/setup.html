Set up your Mac

The dashboard does the thinking; your Mac does the buying. One command installs it, keeps it running, and starts it again whenever you log in.

1 · Install the worker

Open Terminal, paste this, press return. It's safe to run again any time — it upgrades in place and never touches anything outside its own folder.

what it actually does
  1. Installs the worker to ~/Library/Application Support/DealDesk
  2. Builds its own Python environment and downloads a private copy of Chromium (it never touches your Chrome, your tabs, or your cookies)
  3. Writes your server URL and worker token to a 0600 config file
  4. Registers a launchd agent: starts at login, and restarts automatically if it ever crashes or gets killed
  5. Starts it — in dry run, so it does everything except the final payment click
Nothing runs as root. Remove it completely with dealdesk uninstall.

2 · Add your card and login details

These stay on your Mac. This server only ever learns the last 4 digits, the limits you type into the Cards page, and whether a merchant session is alive.

dealdesk vault
Opens a local editor at 127.0.0.1:8765 — bound to localhost, nothing on your network can reach it.

3 · Sign in to each merchant, once

A browser opens, you sign in by hand (2FA and all), and the saved profile keeps you signed in from then on. Only needed for merchants where checkout requires an account.

dealdesk login bestbuy.com

4 · When you're ready to actually buy

The worker installs in dry run: it adds to cart, fills everything in, reaches the review page, and stops. Two separate switches have to be on before money moves — this one on your Mac, and Auto-buy in the dashboard.

dealdesk live
Off means carts get prepared and parked, and you press Buy on the deal yourself.

Does my Mac need to stay on?

Only for buying. The dashboard keeps vetting links, researching and logging around the clock — if your Mac is closed, deals still appear in the Console, they just aren't purchased. Anything queued for more than 15 minutes is dropped rather than bought late.

To keep it awake while you're around: lid open, plugged in, and caffeinate -dimsu. For real 24/7, a cheap Mac mini at home is the right answer — the worker wants your home connection, not a datacenter's.
